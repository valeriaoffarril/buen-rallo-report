Plain-language glossary

What do these terms mean?

OSAT (Overall Satisfaction)
The share of reviews that are the full 5 stars. In this report we call it "great reviews (5-star)."
CSAT (Customer Satisfaction)
The share of reviews that are 4 or 5 stars — a slightly broader "happy customer" measure than OSAT. In this report we call it "great reviews (4-5-star)."
B3 OSAT (Bottom-3-box)
The share of reviews that are 1, 2, or 3 stars — the customers who were not fully satisfied. In this report we call it "low reviews (1-3-star)."
NPS (Net Promoter Score)
A -100 to +100 score measuring how likely customers are to recommend the restaurant to a friend. Above 0 is generally good, above 50 is excellent.
Overall sentiment score
A -100 to +100 score we calculated from AI-tagged customer comments: (% positive comments) minus (% negative comments). It captures the tone of what people actually wrote, separate from the star rating they gave.
Incident rate
How often a specific complaint type (e.g. "poor service") shows up relative to total feedback volume. A higher number means that issue is more common, not more severe.
Resolution rate
The share of complaint/case-form submissions that were fully closed out and answered, versus left open.
GMB / Google, Yelp, Doordash, Uber Eats
The platforms where customers leave public reviews. GMB stands for "Google My Business," Google's review platform.

Net sentiment scores are calculated directly from the Categories & Keywords Positive/Negative/Neutral tags: (% positive) minus (% negative), not a category-weighted estimate.
